Status: editorial evidence only. This is not a provider ranking, recommendation, quote, or affiliate claim. Re-check policy wording immediately before publication because products vary by province and can change.

Search Console context: the owner-supplied official export (data through 2026-09-18) shows emerging Canadian pet-insurance demand. That prioritizes this review; it does not justify publishing unverified carrier comparisons.

## What the primary/provider sources establish

| Provider | Pre-existing-condition signal | Waiting-period / deductible signal | Editorial consequence |

| Fetch Canada | Signs or symptoms before enrollment/effective date or during the waiting period can make a condition pre-existing; its current materials also describe a route for some curable conditions after a symptom-free period. | Current materials describe an annual deductible, selectable reimbursement and annual payout; illness waiting period is described as up to 15 days, with separate current language for some orthopaedic conditions. | Do not say diagnosis date controls eligibility. | Trupanion Canada | Current FAQ says signs/evidence can matter before the effective date; it also describes medical-record review. | Current Canada FAQ describes 5 days for injuries and 30 days for illnesses; deductible is lifetime per condition rather than annual. | A generic “annual deductible” description is false across the market. |
| Petsecure | Current policy wording defines pre-existing/foreseeable conditions by first occurrence or clinical signs, with or without confirmed diagnosis. | Current wording lists different waits by category, including 48 hours accident, 14 days illness, and longer periods for specified categories. | “Waiting periods differ” needs provider-specific rows, not one market-wide number. |
| Spot Canada | Sample policy says noted, symptomatic or diagnosed conditions before enrollment/during waiting can be pre-existing; current sample also describes cured-condition eligibility. | Current sample policy describes an annual deductible and policy-specific waiting periods/limits. | Province availability and policy version must be captured with the comparison. |
| Pets Plus Us | Current enrollment terms state no coverage for pre-existing conditions whether diagnosed or not, and require medical-history documentation. | Current public materials explicitly warn that waiting periods, annual deductible, co-insurance and benefit limits may apply. | Do not infer exact values from a competitor comparison page; use the provider guide/policy. |
| PHI Direct | Policy wording defines pre-existing broadly around occurrence, manifestation, signs/symptoms, treatment or diagnosis before effective date/during waiting. | Product structure is unusual: its own materials say conditions claimed in a prior policy year can become pre-existing at renewal. | This is a material structural difference and must not be flattened into “all policies work the same.” |

## Publication rules for the commercial page

1. Never present a provider’s marketing comparison of competitors as independent evidence. Use each provider’s own current policy wording for its row.
2. Record source URL, access date, policy/version date when available, province availability, underwriter and whether the value is configurable.
3. Separate durable education (“what is a deductible?”) from volatile comparison data (waiting days, payout limits, reimbursement percentages, pricing).
4. Do not label a provider “best,” “cheapest,” or “most comprehensive” without a disclosed methodology and current like-for-like evidence.
5. Quotes are individualized. Do not publish a national “average price” from a single carrier as though it were the Canadian market average.
6. Affiliate status and compensation must be disclosed if/when accepted; affiliate availability must never influence factual scoring.
7. Keep the guide in review until every comparison row has current first-party evidence and the existing verification list is resolved.
