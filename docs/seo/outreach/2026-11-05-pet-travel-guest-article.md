# Guest article: The booking folder your pet needs before a Canadian trip

## Article draft

A pet-friendly destination does not make the journey pet-friendly. A hotel may accept your cat while the connecting transport service cannot accommodate the carrier you bought. The useful planning tool is a booking folder that connects the animal, equipment and each part of the route.

Start with one page divided into four headings: the animal, the route, the equipment and the confirmations. Keep unknown answers visible. A blank beside “pet reservation confirmed” should remain a task rather than quietly turning into an assumption.

### Build the route before the shopping list

Write every stage of the trip, including the journey to the terminal, connections and arrival transport. Beside each stage, record who operates it. A ticket seller and the operating carrier may be different businesses.

Ask each operator whether the animal can travel on that exact service, whether a separate reservation is needed and which conditions apply. Save the answer with the date and booking reference. If a connection changes, revisit the corresponding answer.

The [Government of Canada's animal-travel guidance](https://travel.gc.ca/travelling/health-safety/pets) recommends checking airline requirements in advance. Treat that as an early planning task rather than something to resolve at check-in.

### Make two different fit checks

The first check concerns the animal. The second concerns the space and equipment permitted by the operator. Passing one does not settle the other.

Record the manufacturer's exact model and selected size, then distinguish exterior dimensions from usable interior space. Keep the measurements beside your pet's measurements and the operator's current requirements. If a product page offers several sizes, save the selected variant rather than only its general title.

Air Canada's [published cabin policy](https://www.aircanada.com/ca/en/aco/home/plan/special-assistance/pets.html) requires a flexible, soft-sided carrier and lists aircraft-related constraints. WestJet's [pet policy](https://www.westjet.com/en-ca/pets) also specifies soft-sided cabin kennels. These statements do not establish acceptance for a particular animal, bag or itinerary.

For a structured shopping comparison, ThePetClub.ca's [cat-carrier checklist](https://thepetclub.ca/guides/choosing-a-cat-carrier-in-canada) brings fit, access and cleaning questions together. Manufacturer instructions and the operating airline remain the sources for the actual product and booking decision.

### Put accommodation promises in writing

Ask the property what its pet policy means for your animal: permitted species, number of pets, any size restrictions, charges and arrangements if you need to leave the room. Save its reply beside the reservation.

Build a separate list of optional activities. An appealing park, beach or attraction should stay optional until its current animal-access rules are checked. A photograph of another visitor's dog is not a policy.

### Budget for timing, not only totals

Make columns for amounts already paid, amounts due before departure and amounts payable during the trip. Add the pet reservation, accommodation charges, equipment, supplies and any quoted documentation costs without inventing allowances for unknown items.

Keep emergency payment arrangements visible. An insurance policy and accessible funds serve different purposes; confirm coverage and claim payment timing directly with the insurer.

### Finish with a change plan

Before departure, review what happens if the animal cannot travel, the itinerary changes or an overnight stay becomes necessary. Save contact details and booking terms somewhere you can access during a disruption.

Questions about fitness to travel, medication or individual care belong with the veterinary team. International entry and return requirements need their own official checks.

A useful booking folder does not promise a trouble-free trip. It gives you a clear record of what is confirmed, what needs checking and what must be reconsidered when the plan changes.
