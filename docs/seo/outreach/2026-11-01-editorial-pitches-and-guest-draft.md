# The First 90 Days With a Cat: A Cash-Flow Checklist for Canadian Households

A new cat changes a household's spending before the first ordinary month even begins. The carrier may be needed on pickup day. A first clinic visit may be booked for a particular week. Food and litter usually come in packages, not smooth monthly instalments. That is why a single “monthly cost of owning a cat” can be a poor guide to the first three months.

A better plan is a dated cash-flow checklist: what must be bought before pickup, what is likely to recur, what needs a quote, and what money should remain accessible if circumstances change.

## Before pickup: find out what is already included

An adoption fee is not automatically just a fee for taking an animal home. Some shelters bundle care and supplies into their programme. For example, the [BC SPCA's adoption information](https://spca.bc.ca/adoption/how-to-adopt/) describes included services such as a spay or neuter, microchip identification, specified vaccinations and an initial carrier-style box for eligible cat adoptions.

That is one organization's package, not a promise about every shelter or every animal. Confirm the actual adoption documents and check which procedures and products have already been provided. Ask about any follow-up care still due. Keep copies of records for the veterinary team.

Use three columns on a page: “Included and documented,” “Still to arrange,” and “Unknown—must ask.” Do not turn an unknown into a zero-dollar line item.

## Pickup day: prioritize safe essentials over an attractive shopping cart

Before you collect the cat, arrange a secure way home, a suitable litter tray, food appropriate to the cat's existing routine, a quiet place to rest and access to drinking water. Check whether a temporary shelter carrier is intended for the actual journey and whether you need a longer-term carrier.

For a permanent carrier, compare the cat's body measurements with the manufacturer's actual usable dimensions, top access and closure instructions. Do not assume every product called “airline approved” will meet the operating airline's requirements. If you are flying, consult the airline before purchase.

Make a second column for optional items such as extra toys or decorative furniture. Those may improve the home later, but they should not displace required care or the money allocated to it.

## Days 1–30: write down real consumption

A bag of food is not a monthly price. Neither is a box of litter. Record the date, actual amount paid, pack size and how long it lasts under your cat's real routine. That gives you a household-specific replenishment rate instead of a general estimate.

For example, the calculation is simply *delivered price ÷ days the package lasts × 30*. It is a budgeting calculation, not a reason to feed less than your cat needs. Follow suitable feeding guidance from your veterinarian and the product label.

Ask the veterinary clinic what follow-up appointments or preventive care it recommends for your individual cat, when any invoice would be due, and what the quoted service includes. A clinic estimate is more actionable than a province-wide average for a particular upcoming appointment.

## Days 31–60: turn irregular bills into calendar entries

Now distinguish three kinds of costs:

**Recurring purchases** are food, litter and other supplies that follow your household's actual usage.

**Occasional planned bills** may include care recommended by the veterinary team, municipal registration where required, travel or pet sitting. Note both the amount quoted and the date payable.

**Unplanned bills** cannot be reduced to a guaranteed average. Keep any emergency reserve separate from the cost of routine living. Savings are available funds; they are not the same as money already spent.

Ontario's veterinary association provides provincial [cost-of-care reference material](https://www.ovma.org/), which can help you notice missing categories. It is not a Canada-wide quote and does not tell you what your clinic will charge. Ask for local details before committing to a new plan.

## Days 61–90: look for double counting

A common worksheet problem is paying for the same thing in two places. If the adoption package included identification, do not add the same initial procedure again unless you know it is needed. If a product was bought upfront and you still have most of it, do not count the full purchase price as a fresh monthly bill.

Insurance, if you choose it, is not a substitute for reading the policy. Keep the premium, deductible, reimbursement share, exclusions, limit and claim-payment process in separate lines. A six-week promotional trial is not proof that a future full policy will have the same terms.

Review the three-month ledger with these questions: Which products lasted longer than expected? Which bills were larger than their quotes? Which dates are coming up before the next paycheque? What do we need to ask, rather than guess?

## A worksheet that fits on one page

| Due | Item or service | Included already? | Quote/actual cost | Paid? | Source or follow-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before pickup | Safe transport, food and litter setup | Check documents | Get local total | No | Shelter/store |
| First 30 days | Real food/litter replenishment | No | Calculate from receipts | Track | Household log |
| Clinic date | Care advised for this particular cat | Ask | Obtain itemized quote | Track | Veterinary team |
| Renewal date | Municipal licence, if required | Check | Official municipal amount | Track | City/town page |
| Throughout | Emergency reserve | N/A | Household choice, kept separate | Savings | Personal budget |

A useful budget is not the smallest possible number. It is the one that tells you what is owed, when it is owed and what is still unknown. After 90 days you have local receipts and actual usage, which are more valuable for the next year's plan than a generic national price.

*This is general budgeting information, not veterinary or insurance advice. Costs and included services vary. No veterinary review or product testing is claimed.*
