**Raw pet food has not been shown to be a healthier choice simply because it is uncooked.** The Canadian Veterinary Medical Association concludes that documented risks to animals and people outweigh the proposed benefits. If you want different ingredients or a less processed-looking meal, discuss a nutritionally complete cooked option with your veterinarian before changing diets.

This guide separates three questions: what the health evidence says, whether an individual recipe meets your pet's needs, and where Canadian manufacturers operate. A business listing does not answer the first two.

## What are the claimed benefits—and what is established?

Owners may prefer recognisable ingredients, different textures or control over preparation. Those are preferences, not evidence that a diet prevents disease or improves long-term health. Testimonials about a particular dog's coat or stools cannot establish what caused the change.

The [CVMA's position on raw meat-based pet food](https://www.canadianveterinarians.net/policy-and-outreach/position-statements/statements/safety-of-raw-meat-based-pet-food-products) describes limited evidence of clinically meaningful advantages over appropriate cooked diets. It also distinguishes raw meals from raw treats and toppers: a small portion is still relevant to the safety discussion. “Natural,” “premium” and a Canadian address do not establish the suitability of the finished recipe.

## Two separate checks: nutrition and food safety

**A complete diet can still carry germs; a product with safety controls can still be unsuitable as a main diet.** Ask about both, rather than treating one answer as proof of the other.

The CVMA notes that raw diets may or may not provide all essential nutrients. Check the exact product's species, life-stage and nutritional-adequacy statement, not just the brand's homepage. A complementary product or treat is not interchangeable with a complete meal. Bring its label to your veterinary appointment; our [Canadian pet-food label guide](/guides/reading-a-canadian-pet-food-label) explains the questions behind the packaging.

The [World Small Animal Veterinary Association's raw-diet handout](https://wsava.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/Raw-Meat-Based-Diets-for-Pets_WSAVA-Global-Nutrition-Toolkit.pdf) warns that poorly balanced home-prepared diets can contain deficiencies or excesses, with particular consequences for growing puppies and kittens. It also notes that freezing, drying and freeze-drying do not eliminate all bacteria. Bones may break teeth or obstruct the digestive tract; they are not a risk-free dental-care tool.

## Household exposure matters, even when the pet looks healthy

The [Public Health Agency of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/food-safety/pet-food-treats.html) does **not recommend raw pet food or treats**. Animals without obvious illness can spread harmful germs. Young children, older adults, pregnant people and people with weakened immune systems are at higher risk of serious illness.

Every food format needs careful handling. PHAC recommends washing hands after feeding, using dedicated utensils, cleaning bowls and food-contact surfaces, and following storage directions. Keeping raw food separate from human food matters, but cleaning is not a guarantee that raw feeding becomes risk-free. Tell your veterinarian what your pet eats, including treats and toppers, and describe the household as well as the animal.

## A practical alternative to discuss

WSAVA finds no demonstrated health advantage over commercial or balanced home-cooked diets. Ask about a complete wet, dry or cooked fresh food appropriate for your pet. If home preparation matters to you, request help obtaining an individually balanced cooked recipe rather than adapting a social-media recipe. Pregnancy, growth, illness or suspected food reactions warrant an individual veterinary discussion, not a switch based on this directory.

<Checklist title="Questions for your vet and the manufacturer">

- Is this exact recipe suitable as the main diet for my pet's species and life stage?
- Who formulates it, and what evidence supports its nutritional-adequacy statement?
- What food-safety controls and batch testing are used, and what are their limitations?
- How can I identify a batch, check recalls and report a problem?
- What storage, handling and delivery arrangements apply to my address?
- Would a complete cooked option meet the same ingredient or convenience preferences?

</Checklist>
