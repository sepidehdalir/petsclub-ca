Include your animals in the emergency plan before an evacuation warning, power failure or severe storm. The practical questions are simple: who can collect them, how will they travel, and where can they stay? Write down answers that work for your household.

## Confirm a destination

Do not assume an evacuation centre accepts pets. [Public Safety Canada's pet guidance](https://www.getprepared.gc.ca/cnt/plns/ptsndsrvcnmls-eng.aspx) warns that shelter access may be limited to service animals. Confirm the current local arrangements with emergency officials.

Identify friends, accommodation or boarding providers that could take your animals. Ask about species, numbers, booking, documents and costs. Keep options outside your immediate area in case the same event affects nearby destinations. A provider on a list is not a confirmed reservation.

## Pack a portable pet kit

Federal guidance recommends at least 72 hours of food and water, secure transport equipment, identification, hygiene supplies, medications and records. Personalise the kit with your veterinarian where medical needs are involved. The federal checklist also includes familiar bedding and a toy.

Store supplies where you can reach them quickly. Keep a paper contact list and recent photographs. Check expiry dates and replace consumed supplies. Plan how you will carry the kit as well as the animals; assembling more than you can move does not solve the transport problem.

## Practise transport

Make sure carriers, restraints and leads are available and suitable for your animals. Follow their manufacturers' fitting and securing instructions. Practise loading without forcing a frightened animal. Arrange help if one person cannot move every animal and the kit safely.

Our [travel checklist](/guides/travelling-with-a-pet-in-canada) covers route and accommodation questions. The same preparation is useful for an evacuation, but current official instructions take priority over a planned route.

## Follow local emergency instructions

Monitor your municipality or province's official alerts. Follow evacuation orders promptly. If you cannot take an animal, tell emergency officials or the designated animal response service and ask for current instructions. Do not delay your own safe evacuation or re-enter a restricted area.

Do not assume a sign on the door guarantees rescue. If officials recommend a notice, keep it accurate and tell the designated service directly. This guide does not offer rescue, transport or emergency alerts.

## Make the plan work when you are away

Give a trusted helper access instructions and the information needed to collect your animals if you cannot get home. Agree who will contact whom and where to meet. Avoid putting private access information in a public notice.

Keep identification records current with the relevant registry or municipal service. Our [missing-pet guide](/guides/when-a-pet-goes-missing-in-canada) explains reporting channels if separation occurs.

- Reachable household and helper contacts
- Accessible carriers, leads and transport arrangements

- Copies of records and recent photographs
- A practical route for carrying animals and supplies

For an animal's medical needs, ask the veterinary team rather than using an emergency checklist to change treatment. Update the plan when household members, addresses, animals or care needs change.
