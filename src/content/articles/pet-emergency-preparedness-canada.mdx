Include your animals in the emergency plan before an evacuation warning, power failure or severe storm. The practical questions are simple: who can collect them, how will they travel, and where can they stay? Write down answers that work for your household. This guide is for preparation, not live emergency alerts or rescue assistance. Official sources were checked on October 28, 2026.

## Confirm a destination

Do not assume an evacuation centre accepts pets. [Public Safety Canada's pet guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/policing/emergencies/preparedness/get-prepared/emergency-planning-resources/animals.html) warns that shelter access may be limited to service animals. Confirm the current local arrangements with emergency officials.

Identify friends, accommodation or boarding providers that could take your animals. Ask about species, numbers, booking, documents and costs. Keep options outside your immediate area in case the same event affects nearby destinations. A provider on a list is not a confirmed reservation.

## Pack a portable pet kit

[Public Safety Canada recommends at least 72 hours of food and water](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/policing/emergencies/preparedness/get-prepared/emergency-planning-resources/animals.html), alongside transport equipment, identification, hygiene supplies, medications and records. Its checklist also includes familiar bedding and a toy.

### Is a 72-hour pet kit enough?

Treat 72 hours as a starting minimum in that federal checklist, not a promise that supplies or accommodation will be available after three days. Other guidance plans for longer disruptions:

- [Alberta's pet-preparedness page](https://www.alberta.ca/pet-preparedness) lists 7 to 14 days of food and water, and at least 14 days of supplies when preparing to shelter in place.
- [BC SPCA's current evacuation checklist](https://spca.bc.ca/article/emergency-evacuation-checklist/) recommends two to four weeks of food and medications. This is the animal-welfare organization's guidance, not a Canada-wide rule.

Check your local emergency plan and the needs of each animal. Ask your veterinary team about an appropriate medication reserve, storage and any refrigeration or power-outage needs; do not change a prescription to stretch supplies.

A practical way to organize the plan is to distinguish what you can carry immediately from additional supplies stored at home or ready to load. This is a household planning suggestion, not permission to stay during an evacuation order. Write down where both sets of supplies are, who can move them and what you would do if the vehicle or storage area is inaccessible.

Store supplies where you can reach them quickly. Keep a paper contact list and recent photographs. Check expiry dates and replace consumed supplies. Plan how you will carry the kit as well as the animals; assembling more than you can move does not solve the transport problem.

## Practise transport

Make sure carriers, restraints and leads are available and suitable for your animals. Follow their manufacturers' fitting and securing instructions. Practise loading without forcing a frightened animal. Arrange help if one person cannot move every animal and the kit safely. For equipment you still need, use our [cat-carrier selection checklist](/guides/choosing-a-cat-carrier-in-canada) or [dog-harness fit guide](/guides/choosing-a-dog-harness-in-canada); neither is a crash-test certification.

Our [travel checklist](/guides/travelling-with-a-pet-in-canada) covers route and accommodation questions. The same preparation is useful for an evacuation, but current official instructions take priority over a planned route.

## Follow local emergency instructions

Monitor your municipality or province's official alerts. Follow evacuation orders promptly. If you cannot take an animal, tell emergency officials or the designated animal response service and ask for current instructions. Do not delay your own safe evacuation or re-enter a restricted area.

Do not assume a sign on the door guarantees rescue. If officials recommend a notice, keep it accurate and tell the designated service directly. This guide does not offer rescue, transport or emergency alerts.

## Make the plan work when you are away

Give a trusted helper access instructions and the information needed to collect your animals if you cannot get home. Agree who will contact whom and where to meet. A helper must follow official access restrictions too; an agreed key or collection plan does not authorize entry into a closed area. Avoid putting private access information in a public notice.

Keep identification records current with the relevant registry or municipal service. Our [missing-pet guide](/guides/when-a-pet-goes-missing-in-canada) explains reporting channels if separation occurs.
