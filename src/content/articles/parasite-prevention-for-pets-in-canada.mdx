**Canadian pets do not all need the same parasite-prevention calendar.** Ask your veterinarian for a plan that covers your home region, travel, species and household. Heartworm, ticks and fleas have different risks; a product or stop date chosen for one does not automatically cover the others.

## When should heartworm prevention start in Canada?

There is a familiar Canadian seasonal schedule, but it is not a reason to start, stop or change your pet's medication without your clinic. The [Canadian Parasitology Expert Panel's heartworm guidance](https://research-groups.usask.ca/cpep/parasites/heartworm.php) describes June 1 through November 1 monthly prevention for animals residing in Canada that do not travel abroad. It also explains that prevention targets larvae acquired previously, rather than repelling mosquitoes. Your pet's location, travel and testing history still matter.

The [Ontario Veterinary College's 2025 explanation of seasonal timing](https://www.wormsandgermsblog.com/2025/04/articles/animals/dogs/seasonal-canine-heartworm-prevention-in-canadadoes-our-current-timing-still-work/) discusses temperature-dependent development in mosquitoes and research using weather records from selected Ontario cities between 1996 and 2023. That analysis supports the existing timing in the locations studied. **It does not establish one correct calendar for every Canadian pet or a dog travelling in the United States.**

Before buying a multi-month supply, tell your clinic where your pet will actually be, including a winter trip, cottage stay or recent adoption from another country. Ask for the first dose, final dose, testing plan and missed-dose instructions in writing. If you have missed a dose, contact the clinic instead of improvising a catch-up schedule.

## Does my dog need tick prevention in autumn or winter?

Autumn is not an automatic stopping point. The [Canadian Veterinary Medical Association's tick-activity guidance](https://www.canadianveterinarians.net/media/qlvd05gw/2025_ntam_tick-activity-calendar_en.pdf) says ticks can be active when temperatures reach 4°C and some regions can have suitable days in every month. That is a warning about possible exposure, not a thermostat rule for starting and stopping medication.

Ask your clinic to cover the entire local risk period and the places you visit. A city walk, wooded trail and out-of-province holiday are different details to include in that conversation. Keep checking your pet after outdoor time even when it is already using a preventive product.

The [Public Health Agency of Canada's Lyme disease risk page](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/diseases/lyme-disease/prevention-lyme-disease.html) links current regional information and explains that blacklegged ticks can occur outside known risk areas. Use the map as context, not a diagnosis or proof that your dog needs no protection. It concerns Lyme disease risk, not every tick species or every parasite your pet could encounter.

## Why Vancouver and Winnipeg may get different advice

The [Canadian Parasitology Expert Panel's Western Canada guidance](https://research-groups.usask.ca/cpep/parasitedata/western-canada.php) describes substantial differences within the region. Its flea section notes almost year-round risk in the Lower Mainland of British Columbia, while reporting a different pattern on the prairies and in eastern BC. This regional reference was published in 2019, not a new 2026 prevalence survey; your veterinarian's current local assessment is important.

The useful question is not “What does every Canadian dog take?” It is “Which parasites are relevant for this animal, here, and on our planned trips?” Bring your actual destinations rather than just saying that you travel.

## Fleas: plan for the household, not just the animal

[Health Canada's flea guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/pest-control-tips/fleas.html) explains that immature flea stages can be found around sleeping areas and that managing an infestation involves both the pet and the areas it uses. It recommends measures such as washing bedding and vacuuming carpets and upholstered furniture. A cold day outside is not evidence that an indoor infestation has gone away.

If you suspect fleas, describe every pet in the home to your veterinarian and ask which animals need treatment and how to manage their shared environment. Do not assume one product can be applied to every species, or that adding another product will make the first work faster.

## Can I use a dog's flea or tick product on my cat?

**Do not use a dog-labelled product on a cat.** Species, age, weight and label restrictions matter. [Health Canada's pet-pesticide safety advice](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/pesticides-pest-management/fact-sheets-other-resources/keep-your-pets-safe-when-using-pesticides.html) stresses choosing an appropriate product and following its directions, rather than mixing products or making homemade pesticides.

Permethrin is a particular concern in mixed dog-and-cat homes. [Health Canada's spot-on labelling guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/pesticides-pest-management/policies-guidelines/label-improvements-spot-on-pesticides-companion-animals.html) warns that close contact with recently treated dogs can seriously harm cats. Ask the veterinarian about the exact product and required separation before using it. Do not guess a waiting period.

<VetNote>
If a pet becomes unwell after a parasite product, contact a veterinarian promptly and give the product name and application time. Tremors, twitching, unsteadiness or seizures need urgent veterinary attention. Do not add another treatment while waiting for advice.
</VetNote>

## What should I do if I find an attached tick?

Follow [PHAC's tick-removal guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/public-health/services/diseases/lyme-disease/removing-submitting-ticks-testing.html): use clean fine-tipped tweezers, grasp close to the skin, and pull steadily upward without twisting or crushing. If using a tick-removal device, follow its instructions. Do not use heat, petroleum jelly or other methods intended to make it detach by itself. Contact your veterinarian if you cannot safely remove it or have concerns about your pet.

Record the date and place of exposure. Your clinic can tell you whether identification or follow-up is useful; finding a tick is not itself a diagnosis of disease.

## Compare prevention plans without comparing unlike products

A low package price does not tell you whether two plans cover the same parasites or the same length of time. Ask the clinic to separate the consultation, any testing and the prescribed product in its estimate. Confirm the number of doses, the intended months of coverage and whether the quote is for this pet's current weight and species. This is a comparison checklist, not a claim that the cheapest plan is the right one.
