**Daily brushing is an important part of dental home care, but it is not the only evidence-backed option and it does not replace veterinary assessment.** Choose products for the individual dog or cat, check the exact claim on the Veterinary Oral Health Council list, and ask your veterinary team how home care fits the animal's current dental condition.

## A normal appetite does not rule out a dental problem

Some pets show few outward signs. Changes worth discussing with your clinic include persistent bad breath, drooling, red or swollen gums, blood on a chew toy, dropping food, chewing on one side or facial swelling. These observations do not identify the cause by themselves. [AAHA's owner guide](https://www.aaha.org/resources/your-pets-dental-care/) explains why regular assessment matters even when changes are subtle.

<VetNote>
Contact your veterinary team about mouth pain, swelling, bleeding or difficulty eating rather than trying to solve the problem with a new chew or toothpaste. Ask how urgently the pet should be seen. Do not force the mouth open or keep brushing an area that appears painful. AAHA notes that brushing inflamed gums can hurt and create aversion; ask the clinic when and how home care should start or resume.
</VetNote>

## Brushing: useful prevention, not tartar removal

[AAHA's home oral-hygiene guidance](https://www.aaha.org/resources/2019-aaha-dental-care-guidelines-for-dogs-and-cats/recommending-products/) distinguishes plaque from hardened calculus, also called tartar. Brushing removes plaque; it is not a way to remove established calculus at home. AAHA recommends daily brushing and stresses that even daily care does not eliminate the need for veterinary examinations and treatment when indicated.

Ask your veterinary team to demonstrate a suitable brush, pet toothpaste and introduction routine. Make the plan specific enough to follow: which surfaces, which product, how often, and what would mean you should stop and call. Do not scrape teeth yourself or improvise a treatment for a sore mouth.

If brushing is not currently manageable, say so. [Cornell's veterinary guidance for dogs](https://www.vet.cornell.edu/departments-centers-and-institutes/riney-canine-health-center/canine-health-information/dental-disease-and-home-dental-care) discusses dental diets, chews and other products as additional options, including when brushing is not possible. That is a reason to agree on a realistic plan, not to assume that every dental-labelled product is equivalent.

## How to use the VOHC list before buying

The [Veterinary Oral Health Council's Seal of Acceptance](https://vohc.org/earn-the-vohc-seal/) concerns evidence for helping control plaque and/or tartar accumulation. It is not a promise to cure existing dental disease or an individual recommendation for your pet.

Open the [current VOHC accepted-products list](https://vohc.org/accepted-products/), then check:

1. **Species:** use the dog or cat listing as appropriate. Do not transfer a listing for one species to the other.
2. **Exact product:** match the full product name and any listed size or version, not just the brand logo.
3. **Accepted claim:** note whether it concerns plaque, tartar or both. Do not expand a tartar claim into a claim about every oral-health outcome.
4. **Purchase route:** the list distinguishes consumer and veterinary availability. A listing does not establish Canadian stock, price or delivery.
5. **Package directions:** check the intended age and weight range, feeding directions and supervision instructions for the product actually sold to you. Resolve a mismatch with the manufacturer or veterinary team before purchase.

The list is a useful evidence screen, not a ranked shopping basket. Its links are ordinary reference links here; we are not claiming an affiliate relationship or recommending a particular dental product.

### A shortlist you can take to the clinic

For each candidate, record these fields. Leave unknown answers blank rather than filling them from a similarly named product.

- Exact product, species, package size and date checked
- VOHC entry and its plaque/tartar claim, if listed
- Intended animal age/weight and your unanswered suitability questions
- Ingredients and feeding directions to discuss if your pet has a prescribed diet or food restrictions
- Calories per item or serving, when supplied on the label
- How the product would fit the home-care plan, rather than simply adding another treat
- Canadian seller, delivered cost, return conditions and whether you can buy it consistently

For a dog that gulps chews, has a painful mouth or cannot use a proposed item comfortably, ask for another approach. Cornell cautions that hard objects such as bones, antlers and hard manufactured toys can cause tooth fractures. A product's marketing category is not a safety guarantee; this guide offers no home hardness test that can certify a chew.

## What professional dental care adds

A visible check of the mouth and a comprehensive dental procedure are different stages of care. The [Canadian Veterinary Medical Association's dentistry position](https://www.canadianveterinarians.net/policy-and-outreach/position-statements/statements/veterinary-dentistry) describes veterinary assessment, oral charting, intraoral radiographs and treatment. For companion-animal professional cleaning, it supports general anaesthesia with intubation and intraoral radiographs.

CVMA does not condone anaesthesia-free dental scaling. Removing visible deposits is not a substitute for assessing and treating disease below the gumline. None of this makes anaesthesia risk-free: ask how the team assesses this particular animal and monitors the procedure and recovery. Do not infer an individual treatment plan from a general article.

## Compare the plan, not just the headline estimate

Ask for a written estimate and identify what is included before comparing two totals. A useful conversation covers:

- Examination and any pre-anaesthetic assessment recommended for this pet
- Dental imaging, charting, cleaning and monitoring
- What findings could change the plan or require extractions
- How additional treatment is authorised and how the clinic will reach you
- Pain management, written discharge instructions and follow-up arrangements
- Who to contact if recovery does not match the clinic's instructions

Get fasting, medication, collection and home-care directions from the treating clinic; this guide does not supply a universal schedule. If insurance matters, ask the insurer about the specific proposed treatment and policy terms rather than assuming all dental work is covered.

## The next useful step

If there is a new mouth concern, call the clinic. Otherwise, review the existing home-care plan: what is practical, which exact products have relevant evidence, and when the veterinary team wants to reassess. A manageable routine and a clear follow-up plan are more useful than an untested “best dental chew” ranking.
