Choose a cat scratcher by checking three things in order: how your cat already scratches, how much usable scratching surface the product provides, and where it will fit securely in your home. A tall tower, a vertical post and a horizontal board are different purchases, even when a shop lists them together.

This guide includes a shopping worksheet and two manufacturer-specified examples. It is not a tested “best products” ranking. Sources and Canadian retail listings were checked on October 18, 2026; current stock and delivery still need checking for your address.

## Observe before you order

Notice the direction, surface and location your cat chooses during ordinary scratching. You do not need to move the cat's paws or make it demonstrate a stretch for a measurement.

The [Feline Veterinary Medical Association's Cat Friendly Homes guidance](https://catfriendly.com/be-a-cat-friendly-caregiver/living-clawed-cat/) describes scratching as normal behaviour and recommends suitable choices of texture and orientation. A vertical post needs enough height for the individual cat to stretch; a cat scratching carpet may prefer a horizontal option. There is no single height or material that establishes a fit for every cat.

Make a short observation note:

- Does the cat reach up, scratch along the floor, or use both directions?
- Which surfaces get used voluntarily?
- Where does scratching happen: beside a resting place, near the sofa, or elsewhere?
- Which existing scratcher is used, and what is different about one that is ignored?

Use that note to narrow the first purchase. An appealing photograph or a “large cat” label cannot answer those questions for your household.

## Measure the usable surface, not just the tower

A listing's overall height may include platforms, a bed, decorative parts or sections the cat cannot scratch. Ask for the uninterrupted scratching height of a vertical post and the usable length and width of a horizontal pad. If those details are missing, keep the field marked unknown.

Separate three measurements:

1. **Overall size:** the assembled product's external dimensions.
2. **Usable scratching area:** the surface available for the action your cat prefers.
3. **Space in your home:** the footprint plus room to approach, stand and move away.

For a low board, floor length and width may be more important to your room plan than height. For a post, check both the base and the clear space around it. A narrow gap between furniture can accommodate a shipping box without being a useful scratching location.

Trace the proposed footprint on paper or use a removable floor marker that will not damage the surface. Open nearby doors and drawers. Check the location with the household as it is normally used, rather than measuring an empty corner and moving the scratcher later.

## Make stability and assembly part of the shortlist

Check whether the product stands on a base, mounts to a wall or needs another support. Read the assembly and securing instructions before deciding it fits your home. Obtain any required permission before drilling in rented accommodation.

Do not treat “heavy duty” or “no tipping” in a listing as a verified result for your cat. After assembly, check the fasteners and how the unit sits on your actual floor before allowing use. Stop using damaged or unstable equipment and follow the maker's maintenance instructions.

Ask what can be replaced: a pad, a post, a particular mat, or the entire unit. Record the exact replacement part number if the manufacturer offers one. A promise that “refills are available” is less useful than a compatible part you can identify.

Do not give every row an invented score and call the total a scientific ranking. The worksheet is a way to expose missing information. A product with an unknown usable size or an impractical mounting requirement may need to leave your shortlist regardless of its price.

For Canadian checkout, verify shipping to your postal code, the currency, taxes, any oversized-item fee, and who pays return postage. Ask whether assembly or scratching changes return eligibility. Keep receipts and packaging until you have resolved the permitted fitting or inspection process.

## Place it where the cat has a reason to use it

[Cat Friendly Homes](https://catfriendly.com/be-a-cat-friendly-caregiver/living-clawed-cat/) recommends accessible scratching options near resting places or areas already being scratched, and resources in separate locations for multi-cat homes. A new post hidden in an unused room may not address the location your observation note identified.

Do not promise that a purchase will eliminate furniture scratching. Increased scratching or a change in behaviour can need assessment of the cat and its surroundings. Discuss concerns with your veterinarian rather than repeatedly replacing products and assuming a different texture will solve everything.

For the broader arrangement of resting places, play and resources, use our [indoor cat-enrichment guide](/guides/indoor-cat-enrichment-canadian-homes). If you are also comparing food and play activities, our [Digger versus Wave Circuit comparison](/compare/catit-digger-vs-wave-circuit) explains the difference; that separate page contains disclosed affiliate links. Neither a feeder nor a ball track replaces a scratching surface.

Include replacement pads or equipment in your [cat ownership budget](/guides/cost-of-owning-a-cat-in-canada). Start with a setup you can place, maintain and observe before buying a matching set for every room.
